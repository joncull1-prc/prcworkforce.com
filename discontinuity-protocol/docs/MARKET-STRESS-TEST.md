# Stress test against the market

Written as an opponent would write it. The purpose is to find the arguments that
would actually cost sales, not to confirm that the product is good.

Factual claims about named competitors are from general market knowledge and are
marked with a confidence level. **Check current pricing, positioning and feature
sets before any of this reaches a deck or a pitch.** Confidence: moderate on
specifics, high on the structural picture.

---

## 1. Who this actually competes with

Four groups, not one. The product loses if it is judged only against the first.

**Physical habit journals.** Clear Habit Journal (Baronfig with James Clear),
BestSelf SELF Journal, Intelligent Change's Productivity Planner, Hobonichi and
the Leuchtturm habit editions. Roughly £20 to £35. They compete on brand,
aesthetics and shelf presence.

**Habit and streak apps.** Streaks, Way of Life, HabitKit, Finch, Fabulous. Free
to about £40 a year. They compete on daily contact and cost.

**Friction and blocking tools.** Opal, one sec, Freedom. These are the closest
competitors on *mechanism*, because they also change the environment rather than
relying on willpower. They are the real threat for phone-related behaviours,
which is the most likely thing a buyer will name in that box.

**Cessation and clinical behaviour change.** Allen Carr's Easyway, Smoke Free,
NHS Better Health, and the commissioned services Second Nature, Liva and Oviva.
These compete on outcomes and, sometimes, on evidence.

And for the employer channel, which is PRC Workforce's actual market: Unmind,
Koa Health, Peppy, Spectrum.Life and the wellbeing benefits platforms.

---

## 2. Where this product genuinely wins

**It measures something, with a named instrument and a defined exit.** Almost
nothing in the consumer category measures anything. Streaks count days, which
counts compliance, not habit strength. A four-item automaticity index with a
pre-declared exit threshold is a falsifiable claim about the outcome. That is
rare and it is the foundation of every other advantage here.

**It discontinues rather than forms.** The category is overwhelmingly about
building habits. Breaking one is served mostly by addiction-specific products, so
a person trying to stop something that is not an addiction has very little to buy.

**It has no app, no streak and no notification loop.** For the buyer who has
already tried and abandoned three habit apps, "we will text you once a week and
otherwise leave you alone" is a genuine proposition, not a limitation. Finch and
Fabulous cannot follow, because their retention model *is* the notification loop.

**It collects almost nothing.** No name, no email, no account, no app on the
phone, erase your record by replying DELETE. Against a category where the
wellbeing app is quietly the data product, this is defensible and demonstrable.

**Structural discontinuity is the honest mechanism.** Wood, Tam and Guerrero Witt
(2005) is about habits weakening when context changes. A protocol built on
changing the environment is closer to the evidence than one built on daily
resolve. **[verify]** the citation before it goes in any deck.

## 3. Where each competitor beats it

**Clear Habit Journal wins on shelf.** A comparable-looking notebook at roughly
two-thirds the price, carrying the brand of the best-selling book in the
category. On a shelf, next to it, this product loses on every visible axis.
Consequence: do not fight for that shelf. Direct and employer channels only, and
lead with the measurement, which does not show up in a photograph.

**BestSelf's SELF Journal is already a thirteen-week structure.** Confidence:
high. "Thirteen weeks" is not a differentiator and should never be presented as
one. The differentiator is what is measured inside the thirteen weeks.

**Apps beat it on cost, on feedback and on the moment of failure.** Free versus
a hardback and a text service. A chart versus one number a week. A nudge on the
day someone slips versus a text next Sunday. The weakest moment in this protocol
is the evening of a relapse, and there is nothing there.

**Opal and one sec beat it on phone habits specifically.** They intervene at the
moment of the cue, which is stronger than a weekly audit for that one class of
behaviour. If the person writes "checking my phone" in the box, a screen-time
intervention is genuinely more likely to work. Say so, and aim the product at
behaviours a screen blocker cannot reach.

**Clinical services beat it on evidence.** Second Nature and Oviva have
commissioned outcome data. This product has an instrument and no results yet.
That gap is the single largest vulnerability and section 5 is about closing it.

## 4. The three attacks that would actually land

### "This is an expensive notebook and a text message."

The hardest and the fairest. A 128-page buckram-cased A5 hardback plus thirteen
texts is a real cost base, but the buyer is comparing perceived value, not cost.

The only durable answer is outcome data. Until published numbers exist, the
product is arguing from mechanism against competitors arguing from brand, and
brand wins that argument every time.

Interim answer, which is honest and works: sell the *measurement*, not the
notebook. "You will know, in a number, whether the habit is weakening. Nothing
else you can buy will tell you that."

### "Your own evidence doesn't say what you say it does."

The 1.0 draft was already exposed here: it misnamed its instrument. That is
fixed. Two live risks remain.

- **The 66-day figure.** Lally et al. (2010) is usually quoted as "66 days to
  form a habit". The actual finding is a median with a range from roughly 18 to
  254 days, it is about formation rather than discontinuation, and the sample was
  small. Anyone informed enough to be a natural customer knows this. Cite it
  accurately or not at all. **[verify]**
- **Self-report.** The SRBAI is self-reported. A participant who wants the number
  to fall can make it fall. The protocol has no objective measure. Say this
  openly on page 6 of the ledger. Volunteering the limitation is worth more than
  the objection it prevents, and the competitor set will not do the same.

### "Most of your customers fail your own test."

The inherited exit threshold is 8 out of 28, a mean of 2 on a 1 to 7 scale. If
the typical participant finishes at 12, then most paying customers complete
thirteen weeks of honest work and are formally told they did not pass. That is a
refund queue and a review problem, and it is a product decision, not a bug.

Two fixes, both implemented or specified:

1. **Set the threshold from pilot data, not a round number.** It is now
   configuration (`GRADUATION_THRESHOLD`), so it can be set from evidence. Fix
   the definition before the first cohort and publish it, so a later change
   cannot look like moving the goalposts.
2. **Define a second, reachable outcome in advance.** "Substantial reduction",
   for example a fall of a pre-declared size from the Day 0 baseline, so a real
   improvement has a name. Declare both thresholds before the cohort starts.

## 5. What to change, in order of leverage

**1. Publish aggregate outcomes, and keep publishing them.** Cohort size, median
Day 0 score, median Day 91 score, the proportion reaching each threshold, and the
completion rate including everyone who dropped out. This is the moat. Nobody in
the consumer category does it, it is cheap because the data is already collected,
and it converts the whole positioning from a claim into a record. Publishing the
dropout rate is what makes the rest believable.

**2. Sell to employers, not to shelves.** This is PRC Workforce's actual market
and the product fits it better than the retail one. A cohort licence, a defined
thirteen-week window, and reporting to the employer that is **aggregate only,
with a minimum cohort size below which nothing is reported at all**. Never
per-employee data, and say so in the contract. Every wellbeing platform in that
market has an engagement dashboard, and every employee knows it. "Your employer
cannot see your data, and here is the technical reason they cannot" is a genuine
differentiator that the incumbents cannot copy without rebuilding.

**3. Tie the guarantee to participation, not outcome.** "Complete all thirteen
audits. If your automaticity has not fallen, full refund." It is fair, it is
cheap because completers are the ones who improve, and it drives the completion
rate that makes the published data worth publishing.

**4. Fix the relapse evening.** The known weak point. The lowest-gimmick option
is already physical: the ledger's daily lines. Make the week spread ask for the
strongest cue and what was done instead, so a bad week produces usable
information rather than a blank page and a quiet exit. Resist adding a nudge
loop. It would work, and it would cost the product the one thing it is selling.

**5. Do not use an alphanumeric sender ID.** Operationally decisive and easy to
get wrong. An alphanumeric sender ID is one-way: the participant cannot reply.
Every STOP, START and DELETE in this build depends on inbound messages, so an
alphanumeric sender silently breaks consent withdrawal and the erasure route
while the outbound texts keep working perfectly. Use a UK long code or short
code, and test an inbound STOP and an inbound DELETE end to end before the first
real cohort. **[verify]** current UK registration requirements with the vendor.

**6. Publish the replacement-code procedure before launch.** A scratched panel,
a lost ledger, a code that will not activate. It will happen in the first week,
and the answer should exist before it does.

---

## 6. The honest summary

The product's defensible position is narrow and real: **the only thing you can
buy that tells you, in a number with a published provenance, whether a habit is
actually weakening.**

Everything else about it, the cloth binding, the foil, the no-app posture, is
supporting evidence for that one claim. The build now protects the claim
technically: the instrument is named correctly, the measurement refers to a
behaviour the participant actually named, the same questions are asked every
week, the scores cannot be fabricated through the API, and the exit criterion
cannot be reached by accident or by resubmitting one good week.

What the build cannot supply is the thing that makes the claim worth paying for.
That is the first cohort's published numbers, including the ones that do not
flatter it. Until those exist, this is a well-made product with an argument, and
the competitors have brands.
